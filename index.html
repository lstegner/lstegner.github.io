Keeping people at the heart of high-stakes work.

A research home for students and collaborators who want their work to reach real lives.

Many of the settings where we most need help—caring for an aging population, supporting nurses and clinicians, responding in emergencies—are also among the most complex and high-stakes. Intelligent, interactive systems such as robots show great promise in these settings, but only if they support the people doing the work rather than replace invaluable human contact. Technology that gets this wrong adds burden instead of relief, and erodes the very relationships that care depends on.

This is the focus of the Hearth Laboratory. We combine the strengths of intelligent systems with the power of empowering end users to design, build, and evaluate robotic systems that support real-world needs. We work closely with community partners and stakeholders to bring robots out of the lab and into complex human environments—from senior living to hospitals to emergency response. Our research lies at the intersection of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), and Robotics.

The Hearth Laboratory

Human-Engaged Approaches to Robots and Technology

A hearth is the warm center of a home—the place where people gather. That spirit anchors our lab. We build intelligent, interactive systems that support people rather than replace invaluable human contact, following an iterative, interdisciplinary process grounded in real human needs: we generate design knowledge with the community, develop novel tools and interfaces, and apply both to build and evaluate systems that work in the real world.

  • Understand real needs

    We work closely with community partners and stakeholders to understand their needs firsthand, developing design methods that meaningfully engage the people these systems are meant to serve.

  • Empower end users

    We design and evaluate novel interfaces and end-user programming tools—such as Figaro and Tabula—that allow people to specify and adapt robot behaviors without writing code.

  • Build systems for the real world

    We integrate task scheduling, path/motion planning, computer vision, and more into user-centered systems, then deploy and evaluate them in genuine, high-stakes settings.

Application areas: aging & care · nurse & clinical training · emergency response — and growing.

Our work has appeared at leading venues in human-computer interaction and robotics—including CHI, HRI, and DIS—with support from the National Science Foundation.

If you want your research to reach real people—not just the literature—the Hearth Lab is built for that. I care as much about the people in the lab as the work itself: I mentor each student as a whole person, meeting individually to understand their goals, and I strive for an inclusive environment that supports people from all backgrounds.

Here, you will grow into a researcher who can carry an idea from a community's real needs all the way to a working system. Getting started is simple: reach out about your interests, we will meet to find the right fit, and we will build meaningful research together—toward a future where humans and intelligent systems work in harmony to support people when it matters most.
